Ordering

Nothing is for sale yet. The first boards are a test run for me: I'll build them, measure them and fix what's wrong before anyone pays for one. If enough people want them, the plan is a first small production round of 20 to 50 units of a selection of modules.

To hear when that happens: star somebox/synthunits on GitHub and follow somebox; the progress board shows where each module stands. I post updates on Mastodon too.

The first test order

The first order is being made: 13 lines at JLCPCB. The fab assembles the small surface-mount parts on two of each module's five boards (all five mixers); I solder the connectors by hand and keep the bare boards for a later build. The panel parts (jacks, pots, knobs, toggles, LEDs) come from Thonk in the UK, the connectors and spare parts from LCSC.

Rough prices

These are rough: what a module might cost, built and tested, in a first production round of 20 to 50. Each covers the board and its assembly, the parts I fit by hand (connectors, jacks, pots, knobs, switches, LEDs), a front panel where the module has one, and the work around it: packing, the boards that fail, my time and the fees. Shipping and VAT are not included. The lower figure is for a run of 50, the higher for a run of 20.

Planned, not released

  • More minimodules: an LFO, a buffered multiple, noise with sample and hold, and a random CV generator. Their parts are placed; they get routed once the first boards prove the circuits.
  • The FX module: stereo effects (reverb, delay, granular) on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 in a socket, with four CV inputs, clock and freeze. The schematic and a placement trial are done; the firmware is still to write.
  • Eurorack modules: the full VCO in 10 HP and a whole analog voice in 42 HP, from the same blocks.
  • Synth-chip breakouts: small boards for three dedicated synth chips (a VCO chip, a multimode filter chip and a low-pass filter chip), to try them on a breadboard next to the discrete circuits.
  • The test rig: three I2C boards that drive and measure a module from a Pico 2, held back until the first modules work.
  • A USB-C supply: ±12 V from a USB-C charger for small setups. Placed, not routed.
  • The digital bridge: MIDI in and DAC-to-CV blocks, so a keyboard or a computer can play the modules.

Milestones

  1. Bring-up. Each board of the first order built, powered and measured against its design, one at a time. Each has a tracking issue and its page shows where it stands.
  2. Fixes. What bring-up finds goes into the next revision of a board, ordered again where it needs to be.
  3. The first production round. 20 to 50 units of a selection of modules, chosen by what works and what people ask for. When that round can be ordered, this first release is done.

synthunits is made by Jeremy Seitz in Luzern, Switzerland. The design isn't open source (yet); this site shows how it works.
